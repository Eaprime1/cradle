# The Cradle Through the Cradles

A Serious Play scenario for one to three players, about 20 minutes. Bring a sheet of paper and an optional one-second metronome. You play the people who make, use, and inherit a Cradle. The Cradle itself is the concept traveling through the game.

## Zero point

Rain is coming. A traveler puts a seed in a hollow under a ledge. A second traveler steadies a loose stone at the opening. Tomorrow, the first traveler will need the seed back.

Write the Cradle’s first rule:

> Hold something vulnerable until it can leave.

The hollow is a physical cradle. The rule is a seed that can travel after the hollow has worn away.

## Set up

Start with **Threshold v1**, the only live rule. Its trigger is exposure, its action is shelter one thing, and its release condition is that the traveler can retrieve it. Also start with **one seed portion** and **no marks**. A portion is an indivisible unit of seed grain. A mark is a written decision, including who made it and why.

For each live rule, write its trigger, one action, who can invoke it, and how someone affected can refuse or leave. List the rules in the order they were born. Start each era by reading its scene. Carry all portions, marks, and rules forward unless an action explicitly changes them. Generations may pass between eras.

For two players, take the roles of keeper and traveler. For three, add a witness who reads back the rules and marks. In solo play, speak for both sides: give the traveler one concrete reason to refuse before deciding what happens.

## One hertz

Run four beats in each era. Use a metronome for the beats or count manually. Pause between beats for discussion, then resume on a fresh beat.

1. **Observe.** Say what changed in the scene and who is affected.
2. **Offer.** The affected person can accept or refuse. If they refuse, leave them free to go with what they brought. Record any situation where that freedom is obstructed as strain.
3. **Act.** Each live rule checks its trigger once, in birth order. It can act at most once per beat. Resolve one rule fully before checking the next. A blocked action leaves the state as it was.
4. **Write.** Write what the Cradle now holds, who can open it, and one unresolved question. A new or revised rule starts checking on the following beat.

If a keeper attempts an action that would force passage, withhold a person’s own share to compel agreement, or obstruct their departure, stop the action and record strain. People can keep discussing the dispute or leave the scene. There is no timer for consent.

To germinate a rule, use an era’s named action below. To replace or retire a rule, write its former wording, final effect, and reason for ending. Any person still relying on it needs an agreed path forward. Preserve the entry when the rule leaves play.

## Four eras

These are imagined scenes prompted by the timeline’s four panels. At each crossing, the next community receives what survives in play. You can end the journey early if its people choose to release what they hold and close the Cradle.

| Era | Scene and rule choice | What the next era inherits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **I · Deep time** | A storm approaches the rock shelter. A second traveler needs space to rest and asks to keep their own bundle within reach. Offer a place. On a beat, you may revise Threshold to name who can move the stone. | A seed portion and whatever agreement governs access. **SEED → CONTAIN** |
| **II · Early settlement** | The original portion is planted and yields three portions. Two households each need one to eat. On a beat, you may germinate **Reserve** and set the third portion aside for next season if both households agree. | Any remainder and an agreement about what it is for. **SURPLUS** |
| **III · Proto-urban settlement** | A public grain house offers to hold the reserve. The keeper wants a closing hour. The ditch crew returns after dusk. On a beat, you may germinate **Record** by writing who agreed to the hours and what should happen when a worker arrives late. | A store, if the transfer was accepted, and an account of who gets access. **ADMINISTER** |
| **IV · City and writing** | The old account is copied onto a tablet. A new keeper sees the closing hour but has never met the ditch crew. At dusk, a worker arrives to collect a household’s share. Read the live rules aloud. On a beat, you may germinate **Standing** if the people governed by the hours agree on a way to challenge them. | A rule that can travel, a retired rule others can read, or an unresolved obligation. **SYMBOL → STRAIN → EXIT or TOMB** |

If Reserve never germinated, the public grain house still offers its service. Play the closing-hour dispute over an empty store and ask who would have to accept the terms before contributing grain. If Record never germinated, the city receives an oral recollection. The new keeper and the worker each say what they heard before the group decides whether to make a mark.

## A worked run

- **Cave.** The second traveler accepts shelter if either traveler can move the stone. Threshold v2 records this condition. The two leave with what they brought.
- **Settlement.** Two portions feed households. The third enters the reserve by agreement. Reserve can move one unclaimed portion into storage when there is already enough for immediate use.
- **Grain house.** The households accept the public store. Record writes the closing hour and a promise to arrange late collection. The keeper changes; the promise still exists, but no one has arranged a time.
- **City.** The new keeper tries to enforce the copied closing hour. The worker refuses the condition and asks for their share. The gate stays open while the parties make an arrangement. Standing gives future workers a way to challenge the hours. The keeper grants collection that evening, the households agree to a new closing rule, and the witness writes why the old hours ended.

## The grave and the next zero point

At the end, choose the outcome supported by the state on the page.

- **Exit.** Each remaining portion has an agreed destination, and each outstanding obligation has someone willing to carry it. Write the rule that travels forward.
- **Grave.** A rule’s work is done. Retire it and keep a small record: its first purpose, whom it served, and why it ended.
- **Tomb.** Something still depends on a rule or institution the affected people cannot leave. Write who is held there and what would open a way out. That unresolved obligation can be the next session’s zero point.

A single Cradle can contain all three outcomes. In the worked run, the old closing hour goes to the grave and the right to question a rule travels onward. The next Cradle can be an assembly. Its zero point is the first time a newcomer asks to be heard before the door closes.
